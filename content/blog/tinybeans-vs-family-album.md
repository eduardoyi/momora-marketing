## Tinybeans or FamilyAlbum (or Momora): which fits your family

FamilyAlbum is the better pick if you mainly want to share lots of photos and videos with grandparents for free. Tinybeans is the better pick if you want a private family feed that also works as a dated journal with milestones, and you're happy to pay $7.99 a month or $74.99 a year for Tinybeans+ once you pass 20 uploads a month. Momora, which we make, is a third option built around saving quotes, voice notes and stories as well as photos.

Say it's a Tuesday afternoon. You have a 40-second playground video that Grandma would love to see tonight. You also want to keep the line your kid said on the slide ("I'm the boss of gravity") and be able to find both in 2031.

FamilyAlbum fits the first job. It's built as a [private, central place to share and save family photos and videos](https://family-album.com/en/letter_from_founder), and the free version gives you [unlimited uploads and storage](https://family-album.com/premium), with videos capped at two minutes. Relatives can view and comment from a web browser without installing the app.

Tinybeans fits the second job better. It's set up as a [private family feed with a journal-style memory record](https://tinybeans.com/reasons-parents-love-tinybeans/), and relatives can follow along through email updates and comments.

Momora is a private memory journal that holds photos, videos, text, and voice. You can dictate a quote for up to two minutes, and the app [transcribes it and suggests family tags](/faq/) so you can search for it later. It's newer than the other two and, unlike FamilyAlbum, has no free plan: the family owner pays for Momora Plus.

![A smiling parent on a park bench holds a phone up to speak into it while a toddler stands at the top of a yellow playground slide.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/6ec74305-8919-4a86-be3c-4529bedeccdf/546bc0b6-65bf-4212-9394-00e1be4c7cbd/final-1792487528168.webp "The right app depends on what you need most in moments like this: quick sharing with grandparents, a running family record, or a quote you can find years later.")

In short:

- **FamilyAlbum** is best for easy, free sharing with grandparents.
- **Tinybeans** is best for an ongoing family feed that doubles as a journal.
- **Momora** is best for capturing quotes and stories fast and finding them later.

These are our editorial best-fit calls, based on each company's public pages. They aren't the results of side-by-side testing.

Three tradeoffs decide most choices.

Storage first. FamilyAlbum's free tier takes unlimited uploads if each video stays under two minutes. Tinybeans' free plan stops at 20 uploads a month, which a newborn's first week can burn through.

Then grandparents. FamilyAlbum and Tinybeans let relatives follow along from a browser or email, and our guide to [photo sharing for grandparents](/blog/private-family-social-media-apps/) compares more options. Momora asks every viewer to sign in through the app. For a grandparent who doesn't install apps, that's a real hurdle.

Then privacy. All three are invite-only. Momora's FAQ and privacy policy describe stripping location data from photos and full archive export, and Momora sends some content to OpenAI when you use AI features. FamilyAlbum is clear about deletion, and its policy describes [sharing some data with advertising partners](/blog/is-family-album-safe/). Of the three, Tinybeans publishes the least detail about data handling.

## Inviting family and controlling who sees what

The answer depends on the app.

With Tinybeans, you're the journal owner. You upload the video, and relatives you've invited follow along. Followers can [like or comment by email, website, or app](https://tinybeans.com/reasons-parents-love-tinybeans/), and new followers are [signed up for daily update emails](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/73-how-do-i-invite-my-family-and-friends-to-tinybeans) automatically. You can invite people with a QR code or a one-time link that must be used within 24 hours. That gives a grandparent who lives in their inbox a fairly gentle path. Tinybeans doesn't publish detailed per-person roles, though Tinybeans+ lets you [share full subscription features with one follower](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/22-how-much-is-a-tinybeans-premium-subscription), such as your partner.

FamilyAlbum gives you more structure. Each person is either an [Album Admin or an Album Member](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404247061529-What-s-the-difference-between-Album-Admins-and-Album-Members). Admins invite and remove people. When you upload, you pick ["Share with Family" or "Admins Only,"](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038721473-What-do-the-Share-with-Family-and-Share-with-options-mean-when-I-upload-something) which is useful if you want to keep the tantrum clip between parents. Premium Family and Family Pro add custom share groups. Grandparents can use the [browser version](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038640593-What-s-the-difference-between-the-app-version-and-browser-version-of-FamilyAlbum) to view and comment without installing anything, but browser accounts can't upload, and parent accounts must use the app. [Uploading from a computer requires Premium Family or Family Pro](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038264574-How-do-I-upload-from-my-computer).

You might pick FamilyAlbum for its admin-only visibility and clear roles. You might pick Tinybeans if grandparents would rather reply by email than open a browser.

Momora is the third option, and we make it. Each family journal has [one owner, plus managers and viewers](/faq/). In the playground example, your partner would be a manager who can add and edit memories and invite members. The grandparents would be viewers who can browse, react, and comment. Invites use a private, single-use code valid for seven days, and an owner or manager approves each request.

Momora has real limits here. It has no per-memory audience setting, so it can't do anything like FamilyAlbum's "Admins Only" option, and you can't share a memory with grandparents alone. Its [terms](/terms-of-service/) say ownership can't be transferred to another member. Momora runs on iOS and Android only, so a grandparent who only wants email or a browser will have an easier time with Tinybeans or FamilyAlbum.

## Keeping quotes, stories, and artwork, plus photos

Kids say things worth keeping. Here's a simple way to compare the three apps: can you save those words quickly, attach them to the right kid, and find them again in five years?

Tinybeans is built like a journal. A memory can be [a photo, video, quote, story, or voice note](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/75-how-do-i-add-a-new-memory), filed by date, and Tinybeans lists voice notes among the "special features" of Tinybeans+. It also offers [journal prompts and milestone ideas](https://tinybeans.com/baby/baby-journal-prompts-milestone-tracker-ideas/). The bones line could be its own quote or a caption on a playground photo. [Photo books](https://tinybeans.com/photobooks/) are sold separately.

FamilyAlbum leans toward sharing first and organizing second. The base app is a [private album sorted by month](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038267214-What-is-FamilyAlbum) with comments on each photo or video, so the quote would most likely end up as a comment. Paid plans add more structure. [Premium Family adds Personal Pages sorted by child, with milestones and monthly journal entries](https://family-album.com/premium), and paid plans get monthly and yearly 1s Movie recaps; the [free version gets compilation videos every three months](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404344217113-What-s-the-difference-between-FamilyAlbum-Premium-and-the-free-version-What-features-are-included-in-each-service). Comment search and tags require Family Pro. FamilyAlbum is also the strongest of the three on prints: each album member gets [11 free prints a month, with a $3.99 shipping fee](https://family-album.com/photo_prints), and photobooks are sold separately.

![A toddler in a sandbox talks while a drawn ribbon carries the words to a phone showing text and an audio waveform.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/6ec74305-8919-4a86-be3c-4529bedeccdf/d686af1b-0835-49e5-94c4-8b5aa23a72fe/final-1792487570025.webp "With Momora, the quote itself is the memory: you can type her words or keep the actual recording, and it stays tied to the right child.")

Momora treats the quote itself as the memory. A memory can be words, photos or videos, a kept sound, or artwork. You can type the quote or record her saying it. If you keep the recording, Momora saves the audio along with a hidden transcript, so searching "bones" later should bring it up. You can tag people and [filter by person or feeling](/faq/). Artwork scanning holds up to 10 pieces per memory, and automatic films arrive monthly, on birthdays, and at year's end. Printed memory books and holiday cards are available too.

Momora has limits. A kept sound and the playground photo need separate memories, because each memory is one kind. A photo memory can still have a written caption. Our promise that adding a memory takes about ten seconds is our own claim. Nobody has timed it independently.

## Upload limits, storage, and downloads for photos and videos

Each app handles the upload differently, so the length of that video and your plan both make a difference.

On Tinybeans' free plan, you get [20 photo or video uploads a month and 5 GB of storage](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tinybeans-private-family-album/id521633042), so one playground afternoon could use almost half the month's uploads. Tinybeans+ removes the cap, allows videos up to 5 minutes each, and [includes 200 GB of storage](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/78-what-is-tinybeans). You can download items one at a time unless the journal owner has turned downloads off, and [bulk export is available to Tinybeans+ subscribers and followers granted Full Access](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/59-how-do-i-save-download-photos-from-a-journal-i-follow). When you're ready, [photobooks pull from journal images](https://tinybeans.com/photobooks/).

FamilyAlbum gives everyone [unlimited storage and uploads](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038720993-What-restrictions-are-there-on-uploading-photos-and-videos-Also-is-there-a-limit-to-the-amount-of-photos-and-videos-I-can-upload). Free videos max out at 2 minutes, and Premium Family and Family Pro raise that to 10. On a computer, paid members can upload images up to 200 MB and videos up to 5 GB, and Family Pro saves video at up to 1920 x 1080, versus 1280 x 720 on Premium Family. FamilyAlbum's help center says it [slightly resizes uploads, and downloads and DVDs are the resized versions](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404482216601-Do-you-resize-uploads), not your originals. Bulk download is a Family Pro feature.

Momora puts 1 to 10 photos or videos in one memory. Each image can be up to 20 MB, and each video can be up to three minutes, compressed before upload to fit a 100 MB file limit. Momora re-encodes images to strip EXIF data, including GPS location, so the copy saved in Momora isn't your camera's original file. Family owners can [export every memory at any time](/faq/).

None of the three apps promises lossless storage or print-resolution downloads. If the originals are what you care about, keep a separate backup and use the app for sharing.

## Privacy and control: who sees your photos and how you get them back

All three apps are invite-only. Tinybeans says the photos and videos you upload [can only be viewed by the family and friends you invite](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/9-how-private-is-tinybeans), and invite links work once. FamilyAlbum keeps [albums private to you and the people you invite](https://family-album.com/privacy_policy), and admins can limit downloads to admins only. Momora keeps journals private to family members, with role-based access and media links that expire.

Tracking and ads differ more. Tinybeans' free plan is ad-supported, and the developer's App Store privacy label lists location, contact info, user content, identifiers and usage data as data that may be used to track you, while its Google Play data-safety section says it shares no data with third parties. FamilyAlbum shows third-party ads on the free version, and the California section of its privacy policy (updated October 2, 2026) says it has "sold and shared" personal information regarding cookies with third parties in the past 12 months, with an opt-out. We cover what that means in [Is FamilyAlbum safe?](/blog/is-family-album-safe/) Momora's [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) says: "We do not sell personal information. We do not use private journal content for targeted advertising."

Owning your photos and licensing them are separate things. Under Momora's [terms](/terms-of-service/), you keep ownership and give Momora a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to host, process, and create feature-related derivative works as needed to run the service. FamilyAlbum's terms say photo rights stay with users, with a license limited to what's needed to provide the service.

Momora sends feature-specific data to OpenAI when you use transcription, photo analysis, illustrations, or emotional-tone tagging. Its privacy policy notes that "OpenAI states that data submitted through its API is not used to train its models by default." Momora strips GPS data from photos, though not from video yet.

FamilyAlbum's policy lists encryption in transit and safeguards such as firewalls and access controls. Tinybeans' [Google Play data-safety section](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=com.tinybeans) says data is encrypted in transit. None of the three apps claims end-to-end encryption. Momora doesn't publish encryption-at-rest details, independent audits, or two-factor login for users.

![A parent on a sofa holds a phone showing a list of family profiles labeled parent and child, with a toggle switch below.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/6ec74305-8919-4a86-be3c-4529bedeccdf/343f6a8e-6dd7-4e95-92b7-87900aa78a5b/final-1792487579976.webp "Role settings and download toggles decide who sees family photos day to day, but none of the three apps publishes everything, such as backup retention.")

On Tinybeans, you can [delete your account in the app or by email](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/13-how-do-i-delete-my-tinybeans-account), but Tinybeans doesn't publish how long it keeps deleted data or backups. It does say an account inactive for 12 months (no logins, upgrades or photo book purchases) may be permanently deleted, after email reminders. FamilyAlbum says photos and videos deleted from the app are removed from its servers within 30 days. When a Momora owner deletes their account, journals are hidden for a 15-day grace period and then permanently deleted. Anything you've already exported is no longer covered by any app's access controls.

## What you pay for each app, and what it gets your family

Whether the free version is enough depends on how much you upload and who pays.

Tinybeans' free plan gives you private sharing, [20 photo or video uploads a month, and 5 GB of storage, with ads](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tinybeans-private-family-album/id521633042). Shoot a lot of video and you can hit that cap in a week. As of November 2026, Tinybeans+ costs [$7.99 a month or $74.99 a year](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/22-how-much-is-a-tinybeans-premium-subscription) in the US, and the App Store listing offers a two-week free trial. It adds unlimited uploads, five-minute videos, 200 GB, no ads for you and your followers, full features for one follower, memory export, and free shipping on photo books. Subscriptions [renew automatically unless you turn off auto-renew at least 24 hours before the period ends](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tinybeans-private-family-album/id521633042). If you cancel, Tinybeans says it [won't delete your memories](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/87-will-my-memories-be-deleted-if-i-cancel-my-subscription-or-downgrade), but the journal drops back to the free plan: the newest memories within the 5 GB limit stay visible, older ones are archived until you upgrade, and a journal over the limit can't take new uploads. Bulk export is a Tinybeans+ feature, so export before you cancel.

### What changed with Tinybeans' price

The "price hike" in recent comparisons is Tinybeans', not FamilyAlbum's. Tinybeans' help center says older Family Premium subscribers keep their features, but their [yearly price has increased to the standard $74.99 a year or $7.99 a month](https://tinybeans.helpscoutdocs.com/article/85-i-am-a-family-premium-subscriber-is-my-subscription-still-active); lifetime members see no change, and a legacy plan is canceled for good if it can't auto-renew on the original payment method. The App Store still lists a "Tinybeans+ Legacy Subscription" at $39.99 and $4.49. FamilyAlbum's own blog, in a [July 2026 post comparing the two apps](https://blog.family-album.com/blog/familyalbum-vs-tinybeans-which-is-better-after-the-price-hike/), says the increase was about 87% and that Tinybeans kept roughly 92% of subscribers, citing an interview with Tinybeans' CEO. That post is written by a competitor, and Tinybeans doesn't publish those figures on its own pages, so treat them as FamilyAlbum's reporting. FamilyAlbum's US prices are the same as earlier in 2026.

FamilyAlbum has a free version with ads. As of November 2026, [Premium comes in three plans](https://family-album.com/premium): Premium One at $2.99 a month covers only the subscriber; Premium Family at $5.99 a month or $59 a year and Premium Family Pro at $10.99 a month or $109 a year cover everyone in the album. Family plans add 10-minute videos, computer uploads, child-sorted Personal Pages, and share groups; Family Pro adds HD video, tags, comment search, and bulk download. First-time subscribers get the first month free, and plans renew automatically unless canceled at least 24 hours before the period ends.

Prints are where FamilyAlbum stands out. Each album member gets [11 free prints a month, with extras at $0.33 each](https://family-album.com/photo_prints) and a $3.99 shipping fee. Photobooks run from [$7.99 for an original-size classic softcover to $59.99 for a large hardcover](https://family-album.com/photobooks), plus shipping. Premium Family and Family Pro get free shipping on photobooks and DVDs, but not on prints; Premium One doesn't include free shipping.

We make Momora, so here are its terms in the same detail. Momora Plus is $59.99 a year or $9.99 a month (US list price). Only the family owner pays. Everyone else gets access through the owner. Eligible new owners may get a seven-day trial on the annual plan. The monthly plan has no trial. Printed books and cards cost extra. If paid access ends, the family can still read the journal and the owner can still export everything. Adding or editing memories requires renewing.

For a big family, one album-wide payment like FamilyAlbum's Family plans or Momora's means relatives don't each pay. If you print often, FamilyAlbum's free monthly prints add up. If losing access worries you, look at what you can still read and export after you stop paying.

## Which app fits your family's priority

There's no single winner. It depends on what you want the app to do.

Choose FamilyAlbum if you want high-volume sharing on a generous free tier, or if relatives use a mix of iPhone, Android, and [a web browser](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038640593-What-s-the-difference-between-the-app-version-and-browser-version-of-FamilyAlbum). It's the easiest pick for grandparents who won't install anything. The tradeoff is that the structured, child-sorted views sit behind Premium Family, and downloads come back resized.

Choose Tinybeans if you want a conventional private baby journal with milestones, prompts and photo books, and you'd like email updates for relatives. The tradeoff is that the free plan's 20 uploads a month runs out fast for families who film a lot, and Tinybeans+ now costs $74.99 a year. That cap is a common reason to pick FamilyAlbum over Tinybeans.

Choose Momora if your priority is capturing more than photos: a toddler's quote, a voice note, a drawing, all in one private family timeline. The tradeoff is that it requires a paid owner subscription, with no free plan like FamilyAlbum's, and every relative needs the app. Family owners can export the journal at any time, but you should still keep your original files. Either competitor makes more sense if you mainly share photos or want a free plan.

If quick, structured memory capture is what you're after, [read the Momora FAQ](/faq/) and decide whether it fits. If not, the other two are solid choices.

## FAQ

### How much does the Tinybeans app cost?

Tinybeans is free with ads, 20 photo or video uploads a month and 5 GB of storage. As of November 2026, Tinybeans+ costs $7.99 a month or $74.99 a year in the US, with a two-week free trial listed on the App Store. It adds unlimited uploads, five-minute videos, 200 GB of storage and no ads.

### What are the best Tinybeans alternatives?

FamilyAlbum is the closest free alternative: unlimited uploads, videos up to two minutes, and a browser version for relatives. If you want prompts that build a baby book, [Qeepsake](/blog/qeepsake-review/) is another option (it's owned by Tinybeans). If you want quotes and voice notes as well as photos, Momora is our app. Our [private family photo sharing app comparison](/blog/private-family-social-media-apps/) covers more choices.

### Does FamilyAlbum use your photos?

FamilyAlbum's terms say the rights to your photos stay with you, with a license limited to what it needs to run the service. Its privacy policy describes processing photos for features such as face detection and AI upload suggestions, and its help center says uploads are slightly resized. FamilyAlbum's blog says photos and videos are not sold or shared with advertisers, though its policy discloses cookie-related data sharing. Read our [FamilyAlbum safety review](/blog/is-family-album-safe/) for the details.

### What is the easiest way to share photos with family?

For relatives who won't install apps, a private shared album with a browser or email option is easiest. FamilyAlbum lets relatives view and comment in a web browser, and Tinybeans sends update emails relatives can reply to. Google Photos shared albums are another route; our [Google Photos family sharing guide](/blog/google-photos-family-sharing/) covers how they work.

### What is the best family album app?

It depends on the job. FamilyAlbum is the strongest free option for sharing photos and videos with grandparents. Tinybeans is better if you want a dated journal with milestones. If you want to keep quotes, voice notes and artwork alongside photos, a memory journal like Momora fits better. Our [FamilyAlbum app review](/blog/familyalbum-app-review/) covers its free and Premium plans in depth.

### Can I move my photos from Tinybeans to FamilyAlbum?

Not directly. FamilyAlbum's help center says there's [no way to transfer photos and videos straight from Tinybeans](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404951400089-Can-I-transfer-my-photos-and-videos-directly-from-Tinybeans-to-FamilyAlbum). Download your media first (bulk export from Tinybeans requires Tinybeans+), then upload it to FamilyAlbum, ideally from your original files.
