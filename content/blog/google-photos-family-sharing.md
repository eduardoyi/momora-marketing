A Google One family group shares storage space, not photos. Google says ["your files aren't shared with the rest of your family"](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9004015), and family members can't open your photos, videos or Drive files unless you share them directly. To show relatives your pictures, you share them from inside Google Photos, with a shared album, partner sharing or a link.

Everything below was checked against Google's help pages as of November 2026.

## What Google family members can and can't see

Family members in a Google One group can see each other's name, profile photo, email address and [how much of the shared storage each person uses](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9004015). That's it. Your library, albums, Gmail and Drive stay private.

Google Photos has three ways to actually share pictures. The family group is a fourth thing people often mix up with them.

| Method | Who sees it | Who can add | Updates on its own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared album | People you invite, or anyone with the album link | You, plus others if you leave "Collaborate" on | Yes, if the album auto-adds photos of chosen people |
| Partner sharing | One person | You | Yes, new backed-up photos that match your settings |
| Shared link | Anyone who has the link | Anyone with the link, while "Collaborate" is on | Same as the album behind it |
| Google One family group | Nobody sees your photos | Nobody | Shares storage only |

*Table: Google Photos and Google One help pages, checked November 19, 2026.*

## How to share a Google Photos album with family

A shared album is the best fit for grandparents, aunts and uncles. Here's how on Android, per [Google's sharing guide](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6131416); the iPhone app works the same way with slightly different menu names.

1. Open Google Photos and tap **Photos** at the bottom.
2. Touch and hold to select the photos you want, then tap **Create** and **Album**.
3. Name the album (for example, "The kids, 2026") and tap **Done**.
4. Tap **Share**, then choose who to share with from your contacts or by Google Account.
5. For relatives without a Google Account, use **Create link** or **Show QR Code** from the same Share sheet.

Once the album is shared, check its sharing settings:

- **Collaborate**: while on, people with access can add their own photos. You [can't stop just one person from adding](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6280921); it's on or off for everyone.
- **Comments & likes**: turn them off if you'd rather keep the album quiet. Existing comments stay unless you delete them.
- **Link sharing**: turn it off if you only want invited people inside. If you remove someone who joined by link, turn link sharing off too, or they can rejoin.

### Make the album update itself

You can [create an album that adds new photos of chosen people automatically](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128849). Tap **Add**, then **Album**, then **Select people & pets**, pick your kids' face groups and tap **Confirm**. Share that album and relatives see new photos as they're added. This needs Face Groups turned on, live albums aren't available in every country, and Google notes Face Groups "isn't perfect", so the odd unrelated photo may slip in. Only the album's creator can change these settings.

### What happens when you stop sharing

If you turn off sharing for an album, the [photos and comments other people added are removed](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6280921) along with it. Anything someone already downloaded or saved stays with them. If a relative leaves an album, everything they added leaves too. Albums hold up to 20,000 photos and videos.

## How to set up partner sharing

Partner sharing sends your photos to one other person automatically. It's meant for a co-parent, and [it works with one person at a time](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/7378858). Sharing with someone else means stopping the current partner first.

1. In Google Photos, open **Photos settings**, then **Sharing**, then **Partner sharing**.
2. Choose what to share: all photos, or only photos of selected face groups (not offered in every region).
3. Choose a start date: all time, or photos taken since a date you pick.
4. Tap **Select partner**, choose a person with a Google Account, then **Confirm**.
5. Your partner accepts from **Updates**, under **View invitation**.

Photos go over as they back up. Your partner can turn on saving to keep them in their own library, and saved partner photos [don't count against their storage](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/7378858) while sharing continues. If you delete a shared photo, copies they already saved stay in their account.

## Google family group rules and limits

A Google family group is built for sharing a bill and a storage pool. The manager invites people at [g.co/YourFamily](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6286986), and the rules are:

1. One family manager invites and removes members and can delete the group.
2. Up to [five other members](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6286986), six people total. Google One's [plans page](https://one.google.com/about/plans) says every paid plan can be shared "with up to 5 others."
3. Everyone must live in the same country as the manager.
4. Members must be 13 or older (or the minimum age in their country). Younger kids can be added only with a supervised account the manager created.
5. You can [switch to a different family group only once every 12 months](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/6317858). Managers can't leave their own group.
6. Each member keeps up to 15 GB of personal storage, and anything beyond that [draws from the shared pool](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9004015).

Rule 3 trips up families with relatives abroad: an aunt in Canada can't join a US group, though she can still view your shared albums. Rule 6 matters when one person fills the pool. Over the limit, [Photos stops backing up, Drive stops syncing, and Gmail can't send or receive](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9004014), with incoming mail returned to the sender.

The manager can [turn off Google One sharing](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9004015) (Google One, **Settings**, **Manage family settings**) without deleting the family group.

## Google Photos vs. Google Drive for family photos

For family viewing, use Google Photos. It's built for scrolling, albums, comments and reactions. Switching to Drive doesn't save space, because Gmail, Drive and Photos [draw from the same storage](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9312312).

Drive is better at one job: handing over exact original files. To [share a folder](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/7166529), select it, choose **Share**, add people and pick a role:

- **Viewer** can open files but can't edit, comment or share.
- **Commenter** can add comments.
- **Editor** can add, move and delete files and, by default, change who has access.

Everything inside the folder inherits its permissions. A Drive folder with Viewer access is a good way to send full-resolution files to a photographer or print shop, while the family keeps browsing the shared album.

## Private family apps, if Google Photos isn't enough

Shared albums cover most families. If you want something built only for family, these are the common alternatives:

- **iCloud Shared Photo Library**: for all-Apple households. Apple says a library holds [up to six people including the organizer](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-or-join-an-icloud-shared-photo-library-iph28ac9ea81/ios), everyone can add, edit and delete, and it uses the organizer's iCloud storage. Each person can be in one shared library.
- **FamilyAlbum**: a photo feed for grandparents. FamilyAlbum says it's [free with unlimited photo and video storage](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038267214-What-is-FamilyAlbum), videos under two minutes, and up to 100 members per album. See our [FamilyAlbum review](/blog/familyalbum-app-review/).
- **Tinybeans**: a family journal and visual diary for milestones. See our [Tinybeans app review](/blog/tinybeans-app-review/).
- **Momora**: we make Momora, a private family memory journal for iOS and Android where invited relatives can react and comment. Momora Plus is $59.99 a year or $9.99 a month, and only the family owner pays.

## FAQ

### Can my family group on Google see my photos?

No. A Google One family group shares storage only. Members can see your name, email and how much shared storage you use, but not your photos, videos or files unless you share them directly.

### What are the disadvantages of a Google family group?

Everyone must live in the same country as the manager, the group tops out at six people, you can switch groups only once every 12 months, and storage beyond each person's 15 GB is one pool that anyone can fill. Over the storage limit, Google stops Photos backup, Drive syncing and Gmail. It also doesn't share any photos.

### Is it better to share photos through Google Drive or Google Photos?

Google Photos, for family viewing: albums, comments and reactions are built in. Use Drive when someone needs exact original files, such as a photographer or print shop. Both use the same Google storage.

### How do I share photos and videos with family and friends?

Create an album in Google Photos, tap **Share**, and invite people from your contacts. For anyone without a Google Account, send a link or QR code. For a co-parent who should get everything automatically, use partner sharing.

### Can I share Google Photos with more than one partner?

No. Partner sharing works with one person at a time. For everyone else, use a shared album, which can add new photos of your kids automatically.
