## Is FamilyAlbum safe? The short answer

For most families, yes: FamilyAlbum is a reasonably safe, private place to share kids' photos with relatives. Albums are invite-only, data is encrypted on the way to its servers, and FamilyAlbum says your photos and videos aren't sold or shared with advertisers. The trade-offs are that the app scans faces to sort and pick photos, the free plan shows ads, and some identifiers and profile details go to advertising and analytics partners unless you opt out.

Everything below comes from FamilyAlbum's own [privacy policy](https://family-album.com/privacy_policy) (last updated October 2, 2026), its [terms of use](https://family-album.com/terms) and its help center, checked November 19, 2026. We make Momora, a competing family memory app, so read our take with that in mind.

## Who can see your photos, and what invited family can do with them

Only the people you invite. FamilyAlbum's help center says [your album is "completely private"](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056570294-Is-FamilyAlbum-safe) and photos can only be viewed by you and the people you invite. There's no public setting and no search that turns up your album. You send invites from [Settings, then Invite Family](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038113854-How-do-I-invite-family-to-my-album), either to the app or to a browser version for relatives without a smartphone. You can also show a [QR code on your phone](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052306734-How-do-I-use-a-QR-code-to-invite-someone-to-my-album) for someone to scan in person.

Each person is an Album Admin or an Album Member. According to FamilyAlbum's [roles table](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404247061529-What-s-the-difference-between-Album-Admins-and-Album-Members), members can't invite or remove anyone and can upload only if an admin allows it. They can comment and download by default, and an admin can turn either off. Admins can also mark uploads "Album Admins Only", so only the other parent sees them.

![Laptop showing an illustrative album members screen with a download toggle for each relative.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/98b16211-d929-48f2-97be-76c526e70319/1fca33c0-aa9d-4df1-908e-3ceaf1bd4ba7/final-1792527676548.webp "Illustrative screen, not FamilyAlbum's own: per-person download controls are the setting that matters most.")

If a relative shares too freely, an admin can [remove their account](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038113914-How-do-I-remove-accounts-from-my-album) from the Family Members list. They aren't notified and simply lose access. Anything they uploaded or commented stays in the album.

Turning off downloads won't stop a screenshot. That's true of every sharing app. An invite decides who sees a photo; you still need to trust the people you invite.

Verdict: the sharing controls are good. Invite carefully, and switch off downloads for the uncle who reposts everything.

## Does FamilyAlbum use AI on your photos?

Yes. FamilyAlbum analyzes your photos automatically to sort them, build highlights and screen for inappropriate content.

- Sorting by face. [Personal Pages](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043767034-Personal-Pages) use facial recognition to group photos into each child's page, a siblings page and an Everyone page. People who appear often can land on the Everyone page even if they never joined the album. Personal Pages are a [Premium Family and Premium Family Pro feature](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040187033-What-are-Personal-Pages).
- Automatic picks. [1s Movies](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038852633-How-are-1s-Movies-made) are chosen by who appears in a photo, how faces appear and whether it has comments. [Sticker suggestions](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/30207185698201-What-are-automatic-sticker-suggestions) check that faces are visible, and the monthly photobook suggestion gives priority to your [Favorites](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048327254-When-viewing-individual-photos-videos-there-s-a-star-mark-on-the-screen-What-does-it-do).
- Upload suggestions. The privacy policy lists "AI suggestions for uploading local media to your album" among its services.

![Phone showing toddler birthday photos, with illustrated face circles leading to a child's photo folder.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/98b16211-d929-48f2-97be-76c526e70319/d5707702-a49d-4e2c-8ea4-f6c0fc54d6dd/final-1792527680207.webp "FamilyAlbum recognizes faces to sort photos into each child's page, which means the app analyzes every face you upload.")

The [privacy policy](https://family-album.com/privacy_policy) lists what this analysis produces: feature vectors of faces, face detection results, the estimated gender and age of each face, and a score from 0 to 1 for detecting inappropriate content. It says some of this may be processed only on your device. US state-law sections of the policy classify the face data as biometric information.

The policy also names Anthropic as a provider. It says FamilyAlbum uses Anthropic's AI models only on text, such as support inquiries and translating search queries, inside AWS, and that this data "is not shared with or accessed by Anthropic." The policy doesn't say whether your photos are used to train AI models, in either direction.

Verdict: the analysis is real and mostly organizes your album. If face scanning of your kids bothers you, know that it happens on every account.

## How FamilyAlbum stores and protects your photos

There are three kinds of encryption, and each protects against a different person.

- Encryption in transit scrambles photos while they travel from your phone to the servers, so nobody on public Wi-Fi can read them.
- Encryption at rest scrambles photos while they sit on the servers, so a stolen drive is useless.
- End-to-end encryption means only your family's devices hold the key. Even the company can't open the photos.

FamilyAlbum says [data moving between your device and its servers is encrypted](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048091514-FamilyAlbum-security-and-privacy), its servers are protected by firewalls, two-factor authentication and SSH keys, and it gets regular security inspections by outside agencies. The help center doesn't describe encryption at rest. The policy's Canadian section lists encryption among its technical safeguards for stored data. FamilyAlbum doesn't claim end-to-end encryption, which is normal for photo apps whose face sorting and auto-made movies need the servers to see the photos.

One setting is worth turning on yourself. FamilyAlbum offers [multi-factor authentication](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/26431613683225-How-can-I-make-my-account-more-secure) in your security settings, which emails a code when someone logs in on a new device.

The policy lists Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud Platform as storage providers. Its Australian section says data may go to recipients in the US, Japan and China.

Verdict: solid, standard protection for a mainstream photo app. If you need a company that can't see your photos at all, look for end-to-end encryption elsewhere.

## Who owns FamilyAlbum?

FamilyAlbum is run by [MIXI, Inc.](https://mixi.co.jp/en/company/profile/), a Tokyo company established in 1999 with 2,116 full-time employees as of March 31, 2026. MIXI is the data controller named in the privacy policy. The app launched in April 2015 and [passed 30 million users](https://mixi.co.jp/en/news/2026/0507/52325) in May 2026, with more than 40% outside Japan, according to MIXI.

## What FamilyAlbum collects, and who else sees it

To use the app you only need [nicknames for yourself and your child and your relationship](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056570374-What-data-do-I-have-to-provide-in-order-to-use-FamilyAlbum) to them. Email, payment details and your child's gender and birth date are optional. Beyond that, the policy lists:

- Album content: photos, videos, comments, favorites and photo metadata (EXIF), which can include location.
- [Device and usage data](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058441333-What-other-personal-data-does-FamilyAlbum-collect): device ID, IP address, time zone, usage logs, cookies and advertising IDs.
- Purchase details: Apple, Google, Stripe or GMO process payments, and FamilyAlbum says it doesn't store payment information.

Does it sell your data? It depends on which page you read. The help center says FamilyAlbum [doesn't sell your data to any third parties](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360058441673-Who-can-see-my-data), even as anonymized statistics, and its blog says ["your photos and videos are not sold or shared with advertisers."](https://blog.family-album.com/blog/is-familyalbum-safe) The privacy policy's California section is more specific. It says FamilyAlbum has "sold and shared" personal information regarding cookies with third parties in the past 12 months, including information about minors under 16. The categories it shares with advertising networks and analytics providers include your user ID, IP address, device IDs, whether you have children, your age and your child's age.

That's the sense of "sale" used in US state privacy laws, not handing over your photo library. You can opt out with the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link in the policy or in the app's settings.

Verdict: collection is normal for a free, ad-supported app. If you want less ad tracking, use the opt-out.

## What the free plan costs you, and what Premium changes

The free plan gives you [unlimited photo and video storage](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404467567385-Does-it-cost-money-to-store-photos-and-videos-on-FamilyAlbum-Does-it-have-any-file-type-restrictions-or-storage-limits), with videos under two minutes. The catch is ads. You [can't hide third-party ads](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/45910504655513-I-want-to-hide-ads) on the free plan, and banners for FamilyAlbum's own products stay on every plan.

As of November 2026, [FamilyAlbum Premium](https://family-album.com/premium) costs $2.99 a month for Premium One, $5.99 a month or $59 a year for Premium Family, and $10.99 a month or $109 a year for Premium Family Pro. First-time subscribers get the first month free. Premium plans remove third-party ads and add 10-minute videos, computer uploads, Personal Pages and monthly and yearly 1s Movies.

MIXI lists [Premium, photo prints and advertising](https://mixi.co.jp/en/ir/message) among FamilyAlbum's focus areas. Selling photos isn't on that list.

![Two illustrated phones labeled Free and Premium showing the same baby photo grid, with an ad banner only on the free one.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/98b16211-d929-48f2-97be-76c526e70319/67d7452f-2183-45c8-878f-fdd9bd495f7e/final-1792527677780.webp "Paying removes outside ads and adds features, but who can see your photos stays the same on both plans.")

Paying doesn't change who can see your photos. The ad and analytics sharing described above isn't tied to a plan in the policy, so the opt-out is still the setting to change.

Verdict: free is a fair deal if you can live with ads.

## Kids' photos vs. kids' accounts

FamilyAlbum is built for parents to run albums full of their kids' photos. A child appearing in a photo you upload is different from a child holding an account, and the children's clauses cover the second case.

The [privacy policy](https://family-album.com/privacy_policy) says FamilyAlbum doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone under 13 without parental consent, isn't directed at children under 13 who register themselves, and will delete such data "as quickly as possible" if it finds some. The [terms](https://family-album.com/terms) require consent from a parent for minors to use the service, and let a parent or guardian ask FamilyAlbum to disclose or delete the personal information of a user aged 13 or under after verifying their identity.

Verdict: sensible and standard. Keep the account in your name, and decide yourself who sees your kids' photos.

## Getting your photos out and deleting your account

On the free plan you [save photos and videos one at a time](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038259334-How-do-I-save-photos-videos). [Bulk Download](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038776533-Can-I-save-or-delete-multiple-photos-videos-at-once) needs Premium Family Pro, up to 100 items at a time on iOS, and there's no bulk delete. Keep your originals on your phone or in a [separate photo backup](/blog/photo-organization-programs/), which FamilyAlbum's terms also ask users to do.

To leave, go to Settings, then Inquiries, then [Delete Account](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038529013-How-do-I-delete-my-account). Deleting the whole album is a separate request to support, and deleting the app doesn't cancel a Premium subscription. The privacy policy says photos and videos you delete are removed from its servers within 30 days, and other data is kept only as long as needed or as the law requires.

![Parent using a phone on a sofa, with an illustrated strip of photo frames trailing to the floor.](https://pub-645d6d0081d7424baba0ca52ae362e9d.r2.dev/images/98b16211-d929-48f2-97be-76c526e70319/7038de7b-3845-436d-8e13-7d8a98b4b321/final-1792527683305.webp "Leaving is quick, but on the free plan, saving years of photos one at a time can turn into a very long afternoon.")

Verdict: deleting is clear. Exporting everything is the weak spot unless you pay for Family Pro.

## Is FamilyAlbum the right fit for your family?

If you want Grandma to see every photo without another group chat, FamilyAlbum is a good fit. Invites are private, admins decide who can download or comment, and the browser version means relatives don't need the app. Our full [FamilyAlbum app review](/blog/familyalbum-app-review/) covers features and pricing in more depth.

If you want minimal data collection, no ads or end-to-end encryption, look further. If you want a lasting record with stories and quotes, not only a photo feed, a memory journal may suit you better. Momora is one: it's private by default with no public feed, and our [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) says we don't sell personal information or use private journal content for targeted advertising. ## FAQ

### Does FamilyAlbum use your photos?

It uses them to run the service. FamilyAlbum's [terms](https://family-album.com/terms) say rights to your photos belong to you, and you grant MIXI a license "within the scope necessary for providing the Service." The app analyzes photos to sort faces, make 1s Movies and suggestions, and screen for inappropriate content. FamilyAlbum says your photos and videos aren't sold or shared with advertisers.

### Does FamilyAlbum use AI?

Yes. Its [privacy policy](https://family-album.com/privacy_policy) describes face recognition, estimated age and gender of faces, an inappropriate-content score, and AI upload suggestions. It also uses Anthropic's AI models for text only, such as support inquiries and search translation.

### Who owns FamilyAlbum?

MIXI, Inc., a Tokyo-based internet company. MIXI is named as the operator in FamilyAlbum's terms and as the data controller in its privacy policy.

### How can I contact FamilyAlbum customer service?

In the app, go to Settings, then Inquiries. FamilyAlbum's help center also lists [global-support@family-album.com](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/52547246234905-I-still-need-help-How-can-I-contact-FamilyAlbum-Support) as its customer support email.

### How do I access FamilyAlbum on a PC?

Through a browser-version account. Relatives can ask an Album Admin for a browser invitation and then [view the album on a computer](https://help.family-album.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038777953-Can-I-view-an-album-from-my-computer). Admins manage albums only in the app, but can send themselves a browser invitation as a member. Uploading from a computer needs Premium.
