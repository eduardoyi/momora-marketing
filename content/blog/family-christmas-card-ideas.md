You want to send a card to the grandparents this year. You also have a baby, a toddler, or one of each. Getting both of them to face the same direction at the same moment feels about as likely as a full night's sleep.

Meanwhile your phone holds the whole year. There's the beach trip, the first steps and the spaghetti night, plus about four hundred near-identical photos of the dog.

The family Christmas card ideas below start from that situation. They work with little kids, not against them. There are 25 ideas, grouped by how much cooperation each one needs. The first group needs none at all. From there they build up to a simple staged shot, so you can skip ahead to whatever your kids can handle this month. Every idea works just as well for a Happy Holidays or New Year card.

![A parent on a sofa holds a phone showing a grid of family photos from the year, with a Christmas tree glowing in the background.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/11831a51-9457-4c33-8ace-0de31f6deb68/final-1795448185816.webp "Your card may already be in your camera roll, made up of all the moments you caught this year without trying.")

You may not need a December photo shoot at all. The year you already photographed often makes a better card than a tense afternoon by the tree. Those photos show who your kids were this year, and grandparents usually care about that more than matching sweaters.

By the end you'll have a shortlist matched to your kids' ages and patience. At least one idea needs no new photos. You'll also get tips for finishing the card without anyone melting down, you included.

## Christmas Card Photo Ideas for Family That Use Photos You Already Have

Every idea in this group skips the December shoot. Nobody has to cooperate, because they already did, back in July, without knowing it.

A [year of photos](/year-in-review-photo-book/) also tells grandparents more than one posed shot. A posed shot shows four people in sweaters. A year shows the first tooth, the camping trip, the haircut that grew out.

**1. One favorite photo from the year.** Pick the single best shot, even if it's a sunburned August afternoon. It works because the hard part already happened. Tip: open your Favorites album first. On iPhone, the [Favorites album](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-photos-and-videos-iph3d267610/ios) makes a ready shortlist. Google Photos has its own Favorites collection.

![A printed card reading Merry Christmas, with a summer photo of a toddler in a striped swimsuit, propped on a porch table while the same toddler sits behind it with a bowl of berries.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/f2b30385-5886-41de-bedd-0866dfe110e1/final-1795448143974.webp)

**2. A best-of-the-year collage.** Use one photo per season, or one per month. It suits toddlers well, because a collage hides the fact that nobody held still for any single frame. Tip: go through one month at a time, heart the keepers, then choose from the hearts. iPhone Photos can [search by date or place](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/search-for-photos-and-videos-iph392d77d5f/ios), which helps when "March" is a blur.

**3. A baby's first-year growth strip.** Use a row of [monthly baby photos](/baby-book/), from newborn to almost walking. It works best for babies, since a baby changes visibly every month. Tip: the photos don't need to match. Pick shots where the face is well lit, and the change still shows.

![A row of twelve baby photos labelled newborn through 11 months, showing the same baby growing from a sleeping newborn to standing.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/a4e9ea2c-1c67-4ce8-8fc5-24b2e61b56ae/edited-1795448249770.webp)

**4. An ordinary-day candid.** Think yogurt on the face at breakfast, or a bath-time grin. It works because nobody posed. Tip: crop tight on the face so the cluttered counter disappears.

**5. A summer backs-to-camera shot.** A beach walk or a trail, with everyone walking away. No faces have to cooperate. Tip: choose one with clear outlines against sky or sand, because small shapes vanish against busy backgrounds.

**6. The outtake you already have.** A full meltdown, a sibling headlock, the dog stealing the cake. Grandparents tend to remember this card longest. Tip: pair it with a dry caption like "Peace on Earth (pending)."

![A family of four seen from behind, walking hand in hand along the water's edge on a beach at sunset, footprints trailing behind them.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/d3daddbe-409c-41f7-85ad-5669c653f291/final-1795448151505.webp "When nobody has to face the camera, nobody has to cooperate, and clean outlines do all the work.")

**7. A sleeping-baby photo.** It's the one moment a baby is guaranteed to hold still. Tip: pick a shot taken in daylight near a window. Dim nursery photos get grainy in print.

Before you plan anything for December, scroll back to January. The card may already be sitting in your phone.

## Candid Christmas Moments That Work Even If the Kids Won't Sit Still

These ideas need a new photo but no cooperation. You capture what's already happening. With babies, that usually means a baby in your arms or in a carrier. Toddlers do better with an activity than with a pose. Preschoolers often like having a job, such as "hang the red ones."

For all seven, use burst. On iPhone, [swipe the shutter left and hold](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/ipha42c55cd0/ios). On Galaxy phones, turn on the [swipe-to-burst option](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001353/) in camera settings. Pixel's [Top Shot](https://support.google.com/pixelcamera/answer/9937175) captures a short sequence and suggests a frame.

**8. Backs to the camera at the lights.** Kids stand at the tree or the window, or on the sidewalk facing the neighbors' display. Faces aren't in the shot, so wiggling doesn't matter. Tip: crouch to kid height so the lights frame their heads.

**9. Decorating the tree, mid-chaos.** One ornament per branch, all at toddler height. The activity keeps them busy. Tip: shoot in daylight, when the tree lights don't have to light the room.

**10. Matching pajamas during a bedtime story.** Everyone is on the couch while a parent reads. The book holds their attention, so nobody looks at you. Tip: step back and include the book in the frame.

**11. Snow day wide shot.** Small figures in a big white yard. At that distance, crying faces don't show. Tip: step way back and keep the horizon level.

![Three small kids in red, yellow and teal snowsuits playing far apart in a wide snowy backyard with a wooden fence and a bare tree.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/8aa5d7a8-5b7e-47a9-88bf-e891cb3e3a9c/final-1795448148190.webp "Step far enough back and nobody can tell who is crying, only that it snowed and everyone went outside.")

**12. Cookie baking with flour everywhere.** Flour on the counter, the floor and the toddler. The mess is the subject, so it can't ruin the photo. Tip: stand on a chair and shoot down at the counter and the little hands.

**13. The Santa visit, crying included.** A screaming baby on a calm Santa's knee is a classic card for a reason. Santa has seen worse. Tip: keep shooting after the tears start. The outtake is often the keeper.

**14. A row of feet in holiday socks.** Everyone lies down with their feet toward the tree. Feet are easier to pose than faces. Tip: shoot low along the floor, and let the baby's feet kick.

![A row of feet in striped and patterned holiday socks stretched toward a lit Christmas tree, with the smallest pair at the edge of the frame.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/9ff0c00a-a1e5-4adf-8a6f-24770aafb8e0/final-1795448150488.webp "The blurry kick is the best part, because it proves a real baby was there.")

## Easy Setups That Need About Ten Seconds of Cooperation

These ideas need one short moment of holding still, and nobody has to look at the camera. Set up the spot, the light and the phone before you bring the kids in. Give it a few minutes, then stop.

**15. Parents in focus, kids blurred around them.** You two stand still while the kids run laps around your legs. The blur is the point, so motion can't spoil it. Tip: tap to focus on the parents and keep shooting.

**16. Everyone piled in bed under a white duvet.** Heads poke out of a heap of white bedding. Squirming just rumples the duvet. Tip: open the curtains and shoot from the foot of the bed in morning light.

**17. Baby in a laundry basket with a blanket.** A small baby tucked into a padded basket or box. A baby who can't crawl yet can't do much to wreck this one. Tip: put the basket on the floor, never on a bed or table, and keep an adult within reach. Use a stable basket with no splinters, sharp edges or loose parts. If nobody can stay hands-on, photograph the baby on a blanket on the floor instead.

![Three photos side by side: a sleeping baby in a padded wicker basket on a rug with a parent's hand on the rim, a toddler walking down a path holding a parent's hand, and a girl sitting on the floor holding a baby.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/a5ece06b-f01b-45af-ba5d-7e70f08c8601/edited-1795448243343.webp)

**18. Walking away, holding hands.** The family heads down the sidewalk or driveway, backs to you. It suits toddlers who can walk and needs about ten steps. Tip: have them walk toward something, like a parked stroller.

**19. Big sibling holding the baby on the floor.** Both sit on a rug with the baby in the older child's lap. At floor level, a wobble is no big deal. Tip: sit just out of frame with a hand ready.

**20. Parents kissing while the kids react.** You kiss, and the kids groan, giggle or cover their eyes. Every reaction works. Tip: use burst mode, because the best faces last about a second.

## Simple Staged Shots If You Want One Classic Photo

This group is optional. If you'd still like one traditional portrait with everyone facing forward, these five work with a phone and whatever is in the house. Pair a decent staged shot with a candid backup from earlier in this list, and you're covered either way.

**21. Couch portrait with the phone on a stack of books.** Everyone squeezes onto the couch while the self-timer counts down. Little kids already know the couch, so nobody is perched somewhere strange. Tip: set the timer, run back, and repeat until you have about 20 frames to choose from.

**22. Matching pajamas by the tree.** The same idea as the bedtime-story candid, but everyone sits facing the camera. Pajamas mean nobody has to wrestle into stiff clothes. Tip: put the most restless child on a lap. Lap kids wander less.

**23. Front door or porch with a wreath.** The family stands or sits on the step under the wreath. A [porch with greenery](https://www.shutterfly.com/ideas/christmas-photo/) looks festive with almost no styling, and a toddler can't wander off a doorstep very fast. Tip: shoot on a cloudy day or in open shade so nobody squints.

**24. Open car trunk or the stairs.** Kids sit in a row on the trunk ledge or on the steps, with parents beside them. Sitting slows everyone down. Tip: have a parent sit on the lowest step with the baby so the little one can't topple.

**25. A sign held by a parent.** A [letter board or handwritten sign](https://ideas.hallmark.com/articles/christmas-ideas/christmas-card-photo-ideas/) shows your family name, the year or "Merry Christmas." The sign carries the message, so faces can be imperfect. Tip: give a preschooler a small chalkboard to hold too.

![A family in pajamas squeezed on a couch, one child holding a drawing of a Christmas tree and another a small chalkboard, with a phone propped on a stack of books in the foreground.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/0685d245-e8f6-4fdc-b4d5-e17c16a54abd/final-1795448151813.webp)

Stop after five minutes. Good enough is the target.

## How to Get a Usable Shot on Your Phone

These work for every idea above. None of them needs new gear.

**Wipe the lens first.** Pocket lint and sticky fingerprints make photos look milky and hazy. Your shirt hem is fine.

**Turn off the overhead lights.** Move toward a window or shoot outside in open shade. Soft daylight gives you even faces instead of dark eye shadows and orange skin.

**Shoot after naps and snacks.** A rested, fed kid gives you more minutes. Before a nap you get fewer.

**Hold down the shutter.** Burst mode grabs a run of frames, so you can keep the sharpest one and delete the rest.

**Skip portrait mode with moving kids.** Regular photo mode handles motion better. Portrait mode can blur a waving hand or the edge of an ear.

**Hold a snack just above the phone.** Eyes go up, and the camera is right there.

**Set a timer and quit while it's going okay.** Ten minutes is plenty. Stop before the tears start.

![A parent holds up a phone with a cracker balanced on top while a toddler in pajamas sitting on a rug looks up at it and smiles.](https://img.figurehost.com/images/f1b0d5ec-ebb8-41d5-9cf1-c71b56ee59a5/2c358a38-695b-4554-9765-885eeb9a93e7/final-1795448190288.webp "A cracker held right above the lens gets you eye contact faster than any amount of asking.")

Timing changes with age:

- Babies: right after waking and feeding, when they're calm.
- Toddlers: give them a short activity, like stacking blocks or carrying an ornament, and shoot while they're busy.
- Preschoolers: they can usually follow one simple instruction. "Hug your sister" works. A three-step plan does not.

You need one frame where enough faces are in decent light. If you've got that, you're done shooting.

## Picking the Photo and Writing the Back of the Card

Pick by two things: can you see most of the faces, and is the light decent? A half-smile from a June afternoon in good window light beats a stiff December shot under a ceiling bulb.

For the back, two or three lines is plenty. One specific thing from each kid, one family moment, a warm sign-off. Details like a milestone or a family outing do more than a summary of how busy you were. Something like:

"This year you learned to walk and immediately walked into everything. Your sister started preschool and now corrects our grammar. We spent a rainy week at the lake and loved it anyway. Love from all four of us."

## Family Christmas Card FAQ

### What do you put on a family Christmas card?

One photo, a short greeting, everyone's names, the year. Maybe a few lines about the year on the back.

### What do you write in a family Christmas card?

One-line wish, one update per kid, signed from everyone. "Merry Christmas! The baby learned to climb out of the crib, and the big one started preschool. Love, all four of us."

### What are some short Christmas wishes?

Pick one warm line and sign it from everyone. A short wish fits under the photo and leaves room for the kids' updates on the back. If you send a Happy Holidays or New Year card instead, swap the greeting and keep the rest. Try one of these:

- "Warmest wishes from all of us."
- "Merry everything."
- "Love from our loud little house."
- "Happy Holidays from our crew to yours."
- "Peace, joy and a full night's sleep."
- "Wishing you a cozy, happy season."
- "Cheers to a calmer New Year."

### When should you order and mail Christmas cards?

Order by early December. As of November 2026, USPS says [mail First-Class by December 17](https://www.usps.com/holiday/holiday-shipping-dates.htm); Canada Post, [December 18 nationally and December 11 to the US](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/our-company/holiday-sending-guidelines.page).
