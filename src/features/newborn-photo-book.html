for the first year, which is a blur

A newborn photo book that's done by their first birthday.

You won't fill in a baby book during the newborn months. Nobody does. Save a photo, a video or a ten-second note in Momora when you can, and by their first birthday the best of it becomes a hardcover book of Year One.

Built in the gaps of the first year.

  1. Save the moment, not the scrapbook

    A photo from the night feed, a clip of the first smile, a sentence you say out loud while rocking them. Ten seconds, one hand.

  2. Let everyone add their photos

    Your partner's videos and Grandma's photos from her visit go on the same private timeline.

  3. Print Year One

    Around their first birthday, Momora picks the best moments from birth to one and lays out the book. You look it over and order.

The things you'll want back most aren't photos.

The little snore. The first real laugh. The way they said “uh-oh” at nine months. Save the recording or the video, and it goes in the book as a code you scan to hear it again.

And the words you wrote that week sit next to the picture, so in ten years you'll still know why it mattered.

Behind already? You're not.

Most of Year One happens before anyone has the energy to save it. Add moments with any date, whenever you get a minute, and they land in the right month.

If the camera roll is a wall of near-identical photos, choose which ones Momora may look through and it suggests the days worth keeping, ready to add. Nothing goes in unless you say so.
